Straight hair shows everything. It shines beautifully when healthy, but it can also look flat, greasy at the roots and dull at the ends. A weekly hair mask is one of the easiest ways to bring back softness and shine without an expensive salon visit. These three masks use ingredients you probably already have in your kitchen, and each targets a different problem.

## Before you start: a few ground rules

Masks work best when you apply them correctly. A few basics will help you get better results and avoid greasy hair:

- Apply masks mostly to the mid-lengths and ends, where hair is oldest and driest. Keep thick applications away from the roots if your scalp gets oily.
- Use damp, towel-dried hair for easier spreading, except for oil-based masks, which can go on dry hair.
- Do a patch test on your inner arm if you have never used the ingredient before, especially with eggs, honey or yoghurt.
- Rinse with lukewarm or cool water. Hot water can cook an egg mask into your hair and strip natural oils.
- Do not use any mask more than once a week unless the formula says otherwise.

## Mask 1: Banana and honey for dry, dull hair

This is a classic for hair that feels rough or looks lifeless. Bananas contain natural sugars, potassium and silica, while honey is a humectant that draws moisture into hair.

**You will need:**

- 1 ripe banana
- 1 tablespoon of honey
- 1 tablespoon of olive or coconut oil

**How to make and use it:**

1. Mash the banana until it is completely smooth. Any lumps will be hard to rinse out, so blend it if needed.
2. Mix in the honey and oil until you have a creamy paste.
3. Apply it from mid-lengths to ends in sections.
4. Cover with a shower cap and leave for 20 to 30 minutes.
5. Rinse well with lukewarm water, then shampoo gently.

**Best for:** dry ends, dull hair, and hair that feels rough after heat styling.

## Mask 2: Egg and yoghurt for limp, weak hair

Egg is rich in protein, and straight hair that has been coloured or heat-styled often benefits from a little protein to feel stronger. Yoghurt adds lactic acid, which gently cleans the scalp and leaves hair soft.

**You will need:**

- 1 egg (or just the yolk if your hair is dry)
- 3 tablespoons of plain yoghurt
- 1 teaspoon of lemon juice (optional, for oily scalps)

**How to make and use it:**

1. Whisk the egg and yoghurt together until smooth.
2. Add lemon juice only if your scalp gets oily. Skip it if you have a sensitive scalp.
3. Apply to damp hair, from roots to ends if your scalp is normal, or lengths only if it is dry.
4. Leave for 15 to 20 minutes.
5. Rinse with cool water. Hot water can scramble the egg and make it hard to remove.
6. Shampoo lightly and condition the ends.

**Best for:** limp, weak hair, hair that breaks easily, and a normal to slightly oily scalp.

<Callout>Tip: If the smell of egg bothers you, add a few drops of rosemary or lavender essential oil, or rinse with cool water and a splash of diluted apple cider vinegar at the end.</Callout>

## Mask 3: Aloe vera and coconut oil for frizz and shine

Aloe vera gel is light, soothing and full of water, so it hydrates without weighing hair down. Coconut oil seals that moisture in and has been shown to help reduce protein loss in hair.

**You will need:**

- 3 tablespoons of pure aloe vera gel (fresh from the leaf or a pure store-bought gel without alcohol)
- 1 tablespoon of coconut oil, slightly melted

**How to make and use it:**

1. Mix the aloe and oil in a small bowl until well blended.
2. Smooth it through damp hair with your fingers, focusing on frizzy areas and ends.
3. Comb through gently with a wide-tooth comb.
4. Leave for 30 minutes.
5. Wash out with shampoo, since oil needs a proper cleanse to avoid greasy roots.

**Best for:** frizz, flyaways, and straight hair that needs shine without heaviness.

## Which mask is right for you?

- **Dry, rough, dull:** banana and honey
- **Limp, weak, breaking:** egg and yoghurt
- **Frizzy, flyaway:** aloe vera and coconut oil

If you are not sure, start with the aloe vera mask because it is the gentlest and least likely to cause irritation.

## How to build a simple weekly routine

You do not need to use all three masks every week. A balanced approach looks like this:

1. Week 1: Banana and honey mask
2. Week 2: Aloe vera and coconut oil mask
3. Week 3: Egg and yoghurt mask
4. Week 4: Rest week with just shampoo and conditioner

Rotating masks prevents buildup and lets you notice which one your hair likes best. Straight hair is also prone to going greasy fast, so keep masks off the scalp unless the recipe says otherwise.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Leaving masks on too long.** More time does not mean better results. Stick to the times given.
- **Using hot water.** It dries the hair and can cook egg.
- **Skipping the patch test.** Natural ingredients can still cause allergic reactions.
- **Using lemon juice too often.** It can dry hair and make the scalp sting.
- **Storing leftovers.** Fresh masks contain food that spoils quickly. Make only what you need and discard the rest.

## How long until you see results?

You will likely notice softer hair and more shine after the first use. Longer-term changes, such as fewer split ends and stronger strands, take four to six weeks of consistent care. Remember that masks improve how hair feels and looks, but they cannot repair damaged hair permanently. Regular trims every two to three months will keep ends healthy.

## Final thoughts

Healthy-looking straight hair does not require a long routine or costly products. A simple weekly mask, a gentle wash routine and less heat styling can make a big difference. Pick the mask that matches your hair concern, apply it properly, and stay consistent for a few weeks. If your scalp becomes itchy, red or painful after trying any ingredient, rinse it off and stop using it.
