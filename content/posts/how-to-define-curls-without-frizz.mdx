Frizz is the number one complaint of people with curly hair. The good news is that frizz is usually not a sign of bad hair. It is a sign that your curls are asking for something, most often moisture, gentler handling or better technique. This guide walks you through a simple routine to get defined, bouncy curls with far less frizz.

## Why curls get frizzy

Curly hair has a raised, more open cuticle and a bent shape. Natural oils from the scalp struggle to travel down the curl, so curls tend to be drier. When hair is dry, the cuticle lifts to pull in moisture from the air, and that causes the fuzzy, puffy look we call frizz.

Frizz is made worse by rough towels, brushing dry hair, harsh shampoos, too much heat, and touching your curls while they dry. Fix those habits and most frizz calms down.

## Step 1: Wash gently

Strong sulfate shampoos can strip away the little natural oil curls have. Choose a gentle, sulfate-free shampoo and use it mainly on your scalp, where dirt and oil collect. The suds running down are enough to clean the lengths.

Many people with curls wash two to three times per week. If your scalp gets oily or itchy sooner, wash more often. If your hair is very dry, you can cleanse with just conditioner between shampoos, which is often called co-washing.

## Step 2: Condition generously and detangle in the shower

Conditioner is your best friend. Apply a generous amount from mid-lengths to ends while your hair is soaking wet. Then detangle gently with your fingers or a wide-tooth comb, starting from the ends and working your way up to the roots. Never detangle dry curls, because that is when most breakage and frizz are created.

Leave a little conditioner in, or rinse most of it out with cool water. Cool water helps close the cuticle, which means smoother curls.

## Step 3: Dry with the right tools

A regular bath towel has rough loops that rough up the cuticle and create frizz. Switch to a soft cotton t-shirt or a microfibre towel. Instead of rubbing, gently scrunch your hair upward to squeeze out extra water. Think of pressing, not rubbing.

<Callout>Tip: Do not rub or twist your hair in a towel. Scrunch and squeeze gently to keep curl clumps intact.</Callout>

## Step 4: Layer your products on wet hair

The secret to defined curls is applying products while your hair is still very wet, often called the soaking wet method. Wet hair holds curl clumps together and locks in water.

1. **Leave-in conditioner.** Apply a small amount to add moisture and slip.
2. **Curl cream or custard.** Use this for definition and softness, working it through in sections.
3. **Gel.** This is the step that fights frizz. Gel forms a light cast around your curls as they dry, holding the shape and protecting from humidity.

You can use the "praying hands" method: sandwich a section of hair between your palms and glide down the length. Then scrunch upward toward the roots to encourage curl formation.

Start with small amounts and add more only if needed. Too much product can weigh curls down and make them greasy, while too little leaves them frizzy.

## Step 5: Do not touch while drying

This is the hardest rule, and one of the most important. While your curls dry, the gel cast forms. Touching, fluffing or running fingers through them breaks that cast and creates frizz. Resist the urge until your hair is completely dry.

### Air dry or diffuse?

Both work. Air drying is the gentlest, but it can take hours. If you prefer to use a hairdryer, attach a diffuser, use low heat and low speed, and cup sections of hair in the diffuser bowl without moving it around much. Always use a heat protectant first, and do not use high heat, which dries out curls.

## Step 6: Scrunch out the crunch

Once your hair is fully dry, you may feel a stiff, crunchy texture. That is the gel cast, and it means you did it right. Gently scrunch your curls upward with a drop of oil or a small amount of leave-in on your palms. The crunch will break apart and leave soft, defined curls with lasting hold.

## Step 7: Protect your curls at night

Cotton pillowcases absorb moisture and create friction. Sleep on a satin or silk pillowcase, or wrap your hair in a satin bonnet or scarf. For longer curls, try a loose high ponytail on top of your head, sometimes called a pineapple, to keep curls from being crushed.

In the morning, refresh with a spritz of water mixed with a little leave-in conditioner, scrunch, and let dry.

## Weekly and monthly care

- **Deep condition once a week.** A moisturising mask for 15 to 30 minutes keeps curls hydrated.
- **Clarify once a month.** Buildup from products can weigh curls down. A clarifying shampoo removes it, but follow with a deep conditioner.
- **Balance moisture and protein.** If curls feel limp and overstretched, they may need protein. If they feel stiff and brittle, they need moisture.
- **Trim regularly.** Split ends make hair frizzier. A trim every two to three months keeps curls healthy.

## Common mistakes that cause frizz

- Brushing dry curls
- Using heavy, alcohol-based products
- Skipping conditioner
- Washing with very hot water
- Using too much heat without protection
- Changing products too often and never giving them time to work
- Touching hair while it dries

## Climate and humidity

In humid weather, curls pull moisture from the air and swell. Use a stronger-hold gel and seal your hair with a light oil or serum. In dry weather, add more leave-in conditioner and consider a humectant-free routine if your hair feels dry or crunchy.

## Final thoughts

Defined, frizz-free curls come from consistency and patience, not from any single miracle product. Wash gently, condition well, layer products on wet hair, and keep your hands off while curls dry. Give a new routine four to six weeks before you decide if it works. Over time, you will learn exactly what your curls like and how to keep them soft, bouncy and healthy.
