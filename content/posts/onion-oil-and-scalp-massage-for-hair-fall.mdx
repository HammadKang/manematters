Scalp massage and onion oil are two of the most popular home remedies for hair fall. Walk into almost any home in South Asia and you will find someone swearing by one or both. But do they actually work, or is it just tradition? This guide looks at what we know, how to try them safely, and what to expect.

## What the evidence says about scalp massage

Scalp massage is the simpler and safer of the two. A small Japanese study in 2016 found that men who did standardised scalp massage for about four minutes a day over 24 weeks had thicker hair. The researchers suggested that stretching forces on the skin may stimulate hair follicle cells. The study was small, so the results are encouraging rather than conclusive.

Even without strong proof for regrowth, scalp massage has real benefits. It feels relaxing, it can reduce stress, it helps spread natural oils, and it can loosen flaky skin. Since stress is a known trigger for shedding, a calming habit may help indirectly.

## What the evidence says about onion oil

Onion juice contains sulfur compounds and antioxidants, and people have used it for hair for generations. There is one small study from 2002 in which people with patchy hair loss (alopecia areata) who applied onion juice twice daily showed regrowth in many cases. However, the study was small, and it used fresh onion juice rather than commercial onion oil.

Most commercial onion oils are a base oil, such as coconut, castor or almond, infused with a small amount of onion extract. There is no solid proof that these products regrow hair or stop hair fall. Be realistic: onion oil may improve how the hair feels, but it is not a proven treatment for pattern baldness or heavy shedding.

## The honest answer: does it work?

Scalp massage can be a helpful part of a routine and is low risk. Onion oil is unproven, and it can irritate some people's scalps. Neither is a replacement for finding the cause of your hair fall, which could be nutrition, thyroid, hormones, stress or genetics.

<Callout>Remember: Hair fall that is sudden, patchy or comes with itching or pain needs a doctor, not a home remedy.</Callout>

## How to do a scalp massage

Doing it right takes only a few minutes.

1. Start with clean, dry or slightly damp hair. If you use oil, warm it slightly first.
2. Place your fingertips (not your nails) on your scalp.
3. Press gently and move your fingers in small circles, as if you were shampooing.
4. Start at the hairline, move to the temples, then the crown, then the back of your head and neck.
5. Use firm but comfortable pressure. It should feel pleasant, never painful.
6. Continue for five to ten minutes.
7. Wash your hair after, especially if you used oil.

You can do this daily or a few times per week. Many people like to do it in the evening to relax before sleep.

## How to use onion oil safely

If you want to try onion oil, take these precautions.

### Always patch test first

Onion can cause contact dermatitis in some people. Apply a small amount to the inner forearm or behind your ear and wait 24 hours. If you see redness, itching, burning or swelling, do not use it.

### Application steps

1. Choose a product with a simple ingredient list, or make a mild version at home.
2. Part your hair into sections and apply a small amount of oil to the scalp with a cotton ball or your fingertips.
3. Massage gently for five minutes.
4. Leave it on for 30 minutes to one hour. Do not leave it overnight at first.
5. Wash out with a mild shampoo, twice if needed to remove the oil and smell.
6. Use it no more than once or twice per week.

### A simple homemade version

Blend one medium onion and strain the juice. Mix two tablespoons of onion juice with one tablespoon of coconut oil. Apply to the scalp, leave for 20 to 30 minutes and rinse well. Make a fresh batch every time, since onion juice spoils quickly. Keep it away from your eyes.

## Possible side effects

- Scalp irritation, redness or burning
- Allergic reactions
- A strong smell that lingers
- Greasy hair or clogged follicles if oil is left on too long or not washed out
- Worsening dandruff in some people

If any of these occur, stop using the product and rinse your scalp well. Anyone with eczema, psoriasis, open wounds or a very sensitive scalp should avoid onion oil.

## What actually helps reduce hair fall

Home remedies work best alongside the basics:

- **Eat enough protein, iron and vitamins.** Eggs, lentils, nuts, greens, fish and dairy support hair growth.
- **Manage stress.** Exercise, walking, sleep and relaxation all help.
- **Be gentle with your hair.** Avoid tight hairstyles, harsh chemicals and excessive heat.
- **Keep your scalp healthy.** Treat dandruff early.
- **Get a check-up.** Ask for tests for thyroid, ferritin (iron stores), vitamin D and vitamin B12 if shedding lasts more than a few months.

## How long should you try before judging?

Hair grows slowly, around one centimetre a month. Give any routine at least three to six months before deciding whether it works. Take a photo of your parting and hairline once a month in the same light so you can compare honestly.

## When to see a doctor

Book an appointment if you notice:

- Sudden or heavy shedding
- Round bald patches
- A widening parting or receding hairline
- Scalp pain, burning, redness or scaling
- Hair loss with fatigue, weight change or irregular periods

Proven medical options exist for some types of hair loss, and starting early gives the best results.

## Final thoughts

Scalp massage is a safe, relaxing habit that may support healthier hair, and it costs nothing. Onion oil is popular, but the evidence is limited and it can irritate the skin, so patch test first and use it sparingly. Neither is a cure. The most effective approach is to find the cause of your hair fall, take care of your general health, and speak to a professional if shedding continues.
